Automate the data pull behind a client report and a new problem shows up almost
immediately: since adding another chart or metric no longer costs any time, it's
tempting to add all of them. The report gets longer. It doesn't get more useful.

## Automating the pull creates a different problem

[The labor cost of manual reporting](/blog/real-cost-of-manual-campaign-reporting-for-agencies)
is real, and automating the pull and template assembly is the right fix for it. But
once that part is free, the report tends to grow rather than shrink, and length was
never what made a report useful to the client reading it.

## More data isn't a better report

[A survey cited in one agency reporting guide found that 42.86% of clients in
digital marketing were unsatisfied with their agency's
reports](https://www.swydo.com/blog/client-reporting-best-practices/). Reports built
around what's easy to pull, rather than what the client actually needs to decide on,
are a large part of why. A twelve-tab dashboard nobody asked for isn't a substitute
for the one paragraph that says what changed and what to do about it.

## The cadence question nobody revisits

Reporting cadence usually gets set once, at kickoff, and never gets reconsidered even
as the engagement changes. A paid-media account that needs weekly attention in the
first ninety days doesn't necessarily need it a year in, once performance has
stabilized and the client has stopped opening every email the day it lands. Cadence
deserves the same periodic review as the report's content, not a default that just
carries forward.

## What to automate, what to keep human

The data pull, the formatting, and hitting send on schedule are exactly the kind of
repeatable steps worth automating; nothing about them benefits from a person doing
them by hand. What should stay human is the short, specific summary: what actually
changed, what it means for this client, and what happens next. That's the part [that
needs a person](/blog/why-client-approval-bottlenecks-cost-agencies-billable-hours)
to look at before it goes out, not another automated field.

This is the same tradeoff behind [a repeatable client

steps that don't need judgment, and protect the time of the person making the calls
