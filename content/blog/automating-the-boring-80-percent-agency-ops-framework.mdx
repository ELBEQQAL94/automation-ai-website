Most agency operations work splits into two categories, and the split is usually more lopsided
than people assume. A smaller share genuinely needs a person's judgment. A larger share is the
same steps, done the same way, for every client, every time.

## The test that sorts the two apart

Ask one question about any recurring task: does the outcome change based on who's doing it,
or would two competent people produce the same result every time? If the answer is "it would
be the same either way," it's a candidate for automation regardless of how it currently gets
done. If the answer depends on judgment, it stays with a person.

## Where agencies usually get this wrong

The common mistake is grouping an entire process - "client reporting," "onboarding," "content
approval" - as one unit and deciding whether to automate all of it or none of it. Almost every
process is really a mix: mostly repetitive steps with one or two judgment calls embedded
inside. Automating the whole thing feels risky. Automating the repetitive 80% and leaving the
judgment calls alone usually isn't.

## Running the framework on your own task list

List the recurring tasks for one account over a normal week. Mark each one "same every time"
or "depends." The "same every time" pile is almost always bigger than expected, and it's the
pile worth automating first - not because it's the most important work, but because it's the
lowest-risk place to start.
