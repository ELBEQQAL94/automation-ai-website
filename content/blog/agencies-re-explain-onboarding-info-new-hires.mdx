[Radhika Mandhar's reporting on tribal-knowledge businesses](https://upbooks.io/blog/the-cost-of-onboarding-someone-into-a-business-that-runs-on-tribal-knowledge)
includes a construction firm where a newly hired project manager "spent his first
three months simply trying to understand what the actual approval process was."
Nobody withheld it from him. It just never existed anywhere he could open and read,
only in the heads of people he had to track down and ask, one piece at a time.

## What happens when that one person leaves

The same reporting includes a food manufacturer where a quality control process
existed only in one supervisor's knowledge. After her eleven-year tenure ended,
three former trainees ran their own slightly different versions of her procedures,
and two products failed a client audit within four months. Nothing about her
knowledge was wrong while she had it. It just never survived contact with anyone
else having to run the process without her.

## Why agencies hit this on new hires, not just new clients

This site has already covered what happens when a client's context lives in one
person instead of a record: [the sales-to-delivery
handoff](/blog/sales-to-delivery-handoff-gap-agencies), [a strategist's reasoning
that never makes it into the brief an execution team opens](/blog/strategy-to-execution-handoff-inside-agency).
The same mechanism runs just as often on the other side of the org chart. Every new
account manager, every new designer, inherits the same unwritten process the last
five hires each had to reconstruct by asking around, and at agency turnover rates,
that's not a once-a-year cost.

## Why this is a different problem than client onboarding

[Onboarding new clients without the spreadsheet chaos](/blog/onboarding-new-clients-without-the-spreadsheet-chaos)
solves a different gap, getting a new client's information into the system. This
one is about getting the agency's own existing process into a form a new hire can
read without cornering a senior teammate. [The same instinct already covered for
teaching ClickUp itself](/blog/clickup-wont-fix-a-bad-process) applies to the
process running inside it: a short, specific answer someone can read once, not a
call and a folder of tutorials nobody watches.

## What actually stops the re-explaining

The fix is the same one this site keeps landing on from different angles: the
process needs to live somewhere a new hire can open it, not solely in whoever's
been there longest. That's not a documentation project done once and left to rot,
it's kept current the same way [the record behind onboarding a new client stays
accurate](/blog/client-point-of-contact-changes-mid-project), updated as part of
doing the work, not as a separate task nobody has time for.
