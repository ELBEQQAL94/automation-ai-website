When review rounds take too long, the instinct is to lower the bar - fewer rounds, less
scrutiny, ship it faster. That trades quality for speed, and it's rarely the actual fix. The
slowness usually isn't in the review itself. It's in everything around it.

## The review isn't the slow part

A reviewer looking at a piece of creative work and giving feedback takes minutes, not days.
What takes days is the work sitting in a queue before anyone sees it, feedback getting lost in
a comment thread, or a revised version going back for review without anyone flagging that it's
ready again.

## Where the actual time goes

Every handoff between "ready for review" and "reviewer sees it," and between "feedback given"
and "revision starts," is a place delay can hide. None of these handoffs need to take time -
they're status changes and notifications, not creative decisions.

## Tightening the process without touching the standard

Automatic notification the moment something's ready for review, a single tracked status
instead of a scattered thread, and automatic routing back to the right person after
revisions - none of this changes what the reviewer is looking for or how carefully they look.
It just removes the gaps between each step.
