ClickUp's built-in automations are genuinely useful, and most agencies should use them before
looking anywhere else. "When a task moves to Done, notify the assignee" or "when a due date
passes, change the status" - simple, rule-based, no reason to build anything custom for that.

## What native automation is actually good at

Single-tool, single-trigger, single-action rules are exactly what ClickUp's automation panel
is built for. They're fast to set up, don't require any code, and cover a real chunk of the
repetitive work most teams deal with day to day.

## Where it stops being enough

The gap shows up once a workflow needs to reach outside ClickUp - updating Airtable, sending
a client a specific document, checking a condition that lives in a different system entirely.
Native automations can trigger a webhook, but turning that webhook into "check three
conditions across two other tools before deciding what happens next" is past what the
built-in panel is designed for.

## What a custom adapter actually adds

A workflow adapter picks up exactly where native automation stops: multi-tool logic,
conditional routing based on data that doesn't live in ClickUp, and handling the edge cases
that a simple trigger-action rule can't express. It's not a replacement for ClickUp's
automations - it's what runs the parts they can't reach.

## Deciding which one you need

If the workflow stays inside ClickUp and follows one clear rule, native automation is the
right tool and probably already available. If it needs to reach into [ClickUp and
Airtable together](/blog/clickup-airtable-agency-stack-nobody-talks-about-replacing) or handle
a real exception, that's the point to look at [custom workflow adapters](/services) instead.
