[365Outsource traces failed outsourced design work back to one specific
cause](https://www.365outsource.com/fairspin-casino/strategy-in-house-outsource-execution-design-agencies/):
"the failure almost always traces back to an ambiguous strategy handoff. The
external team gets asked to 'design a homepage' without knowing the conversion
goal, the audience segment, or the competitive positioning." That's framed as an
outsourcing problem, but nothing about it requires an outside vendor. The same gap
opens between a strategist and a creative team on the same payroll just as often.

## Why "internal" doesn't mean the handoff is safer

An external handoff gets treated carefully because everyone assumes something will
get lost. An internal one doesn't get that same scrutiny, because it feels informal,
a Slack message, a five-minute walkover, a brief that already exists in a shared
folder. The strategist who ran the discovery calls knows why this audience, why this
angle, what already got rejected. None of that has to be written down for the
strategist to still know it. It has to be written down for anyone else to.

## The same gap, one step earlier

This is the same mechanism already showing up at other points in the client
relationship on this site: [what gets lost when a deal moves from sales to
delivery](/blog/sales-to-delivery-handoff-gap-agencies), [what gets lost when a
client's own point of contact changes](/blog/client-point-of-contact-changes-mid-project).
Both are information that lived in one person's understanding instead of a record
anyone else could open. The strategy-to-execution handoff is the same failure,
happening earlier, entirely inside the agency, before a client ever sees the result
of the gap.

## Why the cost lands in the same place

A designer working from "design a homepage" without the conversion goal or the
audience segment doesn't produce bad work, they produce work that's correct against
the wrong brief. That gets caught in review, if it's caught before the client sees
it, which means the cost is the same rework [this site has already put a number
on](/blog/client-intake-form-prevents-scope-creep) elsewhere: hours redone against
information that existed the whole time, just not in a place the execution team
could reach.

## What actually closes it

The fix isn't asking strategists to write longer briefs, that's the same
naming-discipline trap [that doesn't hold up under deadline
pressure](/blog/client-feedback-wrong-version-of-file). It's making sure the reasoning
behind a decision, not just the decision, lands in the same record the execution
team opens, so nobody downstream has to track down the person who was on the
original call to ask why. [See how workflow design keeps that context attached](/services)
to the brief itself, not to whoever happened to be in the room when it was decided.
