[Garrett Mullins describes the failure mode plainly](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/automate-creative-asset-version-control-marketing-2026):
"a client campaign launches with last week's copy instead of the approved final," or
"a social media post goes out with the brand's old logo." Mullins puts the
prevalence at roughly one in four marketing agencies affected by wrong-version
publishing, at a labor cost of $2,300 to $5,750 and 6 to 15 hours of combined
creative, account, and operations time per incident.

## Why this isn't a competence problem

Nobody approves the wrong file on purpose, and the team that built the right one
usually did nothing wrong either. The gap opens because "approved" and "current"
live in two different places: a client signs off on a PDF export or an emailed
attachment, and the working file keeps moving after that, picking up another round
of small edits someone thought were harmless. The approval was real. It just wasn't
attached to what eventually shipped.

## Why better naming discipline doesn't close the gap

The usual fix is a stricter file-naming convention: FINAL, FINAL_v2,
FINAL_v2_client_approved. That still depends on someone remembering to rename
correctly, every time, under deadline pressure, which is the same failure mode
behind [why client feedback scattered across email, PDFs, and texts turns a two-day
approval into two weeks](/blog/what-to-do-when-clients-hold-up-approval): a human
convention standing in for a system that should enforce it automatically.

## What actually keeps approval attached to the right file

"Approved" needs to be a status on the file itself, not a name someone typed
correctly. When a client approves, that specific version gets locked, and any
further edits automatically become a new draft instead of quietly overwriting what
was signed off. [The same principle already runs through content-calendar
handoffs](/blog/content-calendar-handoff-breaking-agency-delivery): the record
should update itself the moment a decision is made, not wait for someone to
remember to reflect it.

## Where this fits into approvals overall

This is a narrower version of [why approval bottlenecks cost agencies billable
hours](/blog/why-client-approval-bottlenecks-cost-agencies-billable-hours): the cost
isn't just the time spent waiting on a decision, it's the rework when the decision
that did happen doesn't survive contact with the actual working file. [See how
workflow design keeps approval attached to the right version](/services) instead of
whichever one happened to be open last.
