[Agency Management Institute's 2024 financial benchmark](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/automate-digital-agencies-save-12-hours-per-week-2026)
puts a number on the part of an agency's payroll that never touches billable work:
non-billable overhead, reporting, account management admin, internal meetings,
averages 34% of total agency staff time. On a team where every paycheck should be
funding client work, roughly a third of it is funding something else.

## Where a chunk of that third actually goes

[Databox's 2024 State of Agency Reporting survey across 450 digital
agencies](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/automate-digital-agencies-save-12-hours-per-week-2026)
puts client report preparation alone at 12 to 15 hours a week. That's not one
person's odd assignment, it's a recurring weekly cost showing up on every reporting
cycle, for every client, whether or not anything about that client's numbers
changed enough to be worth a person manually rebuilding a deck.

## Why this isn't a speed problem

The instinct when a number like 34% surfaces is to treat it as an efficiency issue,
people need to move faster, meetings need to get shorter. That's the wrong
diagnosis. Assembling a report by hand doesn't get meaningfully faster no matter how
skilled the person doing it is, [the same math behind how many clients one account
manager can actually carry](/blog/how-many-clients-can-one-account-manager-handle):
the ceiling isn't effort, it's how much of the week is spent on work that has
nothing to do with the client relationship itself.

## What the number actually asks for

A third of payroll going to coordination work isn't solved by hiring faster people
or asking the current team to work harder through it. [The same reporting cadence
that eats those hours weekly](/blog/campaign-reporting-cadence-what-to-automate-vs-what-clients-read)
is exactly the kind of repeatable, non-judgment work that doesn't need a person
doing it from scratch every cycle. Recovering even a third of that 34% back into
billable time changes what [revenue per employee](/blog/revenue-per-employee-overstaffed-signal)
looks like without a single new hire.
