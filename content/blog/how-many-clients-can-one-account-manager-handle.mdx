Ask five agency owners how many clients one account manager can carry and you'll get
five different answers. [Belkins.io's Michael Maximoff caps it around 5, warning that
going past that "usually leads to work overload for the manager, a drop in the
quality of service." Sagapixel's Frank Olivo puts high-budget accounts at 1 to 3 per
manager. Whiskey SEO's Sam Bretzmann runs each account manager at 20](https://databox.com/how-many-accounts),
built around a full day a month per client. Same job title, four different numbers
that don't even overlap.

## What the actual survey data shows

A [Databox survey of 48 agencies](https://databox.com/how-many-accounts) found close
to 70% keep account managers under 10 clients each, while just over 10% run 15 or
more per person. That split comes from agencies making genuinely different tradeoffs
based on account size and complexity, not from disagreement about what "manageable"
means. Sculpt's Josh Krakauer adds the part that
matters most: account managers are at especially high risk of burnout, and the more
of their day goes to day-to-day firefighting, the more likely that ends badly.

## Revenue is a better yardstick than headcount

[Agency Management Institute's benchmark](https://agencymanagementinstitute.com/how-many-clients-can-an-account-executive-manage/)
skips client count entirely and ties capacity to revenue: an account executive should
manage roughly 5 to 7 times their salary in billable business, with very few able to
handle more than $500K in annual gross income no matter how senior they are. That's
useful because it explains why the "how many clients" question doesn't have one
answer. A manager running three complex, high-touch accounts can be just as maxed out
as one running fifteen small ones.

## The variable none of these benchmarks isolate

None of these numbers separate strategy time from admin time, and that's the part
that actually moves the ceiling. An account manager chasing status updates,
[retyping the same client info between ClickUp and
Airtable](/blog/how-much-time-lost-copy-pasting-clickup-airtable), and assembling
reports by hand is spending hours a week on work that has nothing to do with the
client relationship. Two managers with identical client counts can have wildly
different capacity left over for the job they were actually hired to do, depending on
how much of their week that manual layer eats.

## Why the default fix doesn't raise the ceiling

The usual response when an account manager is maxed out is to hire a second one.
That's the same instinct behind [the hiring trap](/blog/the-hiring-trap-why-headcount-wont-fix-a-broken-process):
it adds payroll without touching the manual work that capped the first person's
capacity, so the new hire hits the same wall on the same timeline. [What breaks first
as an agency scales past ten
clients](/blog/what-breaks-first-marketing-agency-scales-past-10-clients) is usually
status tracking and reporting, not client relationships, so that's the first place to
recover capacity.
