A deal closes, and everything the client said during the sales process, the scope
they agreed to, the timeline they were promised, the one stakeholder who actually
signs off, exists mostly in one person's head and a call recording nobody will
rewatch. [ConsultEvo's Evrim Aslan describes the specific failure mode](https://consultevo.com/hidden-cost-sales-to-delivery-handoff-agency-owners/):
a "political stakeholder who never makes it into the project record," verbal
commitments on deliverables that were never written down, scope details that get
rediscovered through repeated client questions instead of read off a record that
should already have them.

## Why this specific gap costs more than it looks like

New clients are the most likely to leave. [Focus Digital's churn data by agency
business model](https://agiled.app/statistics/client-retention-statistics) shows
project-based clients churning at 42% annually with an average lifespan of just 24
months, against 18% annual churn and 56 months for retainer clients. The
sales-to-delivery handoff happens right at the start of that riskiest window, and
[the top reason departing clients cite is a lack of communication](https://agiled.app/statistics/client-retention-statistics),
not price or results. A client who has to re-explain what they already told a
salesperson reads that as exactly this kind of communication failure, even when the
work itself is on track.

## The fix isn't a longer handoff document

The instinct is to build a more thorough handoff template, more fields for the
salesperson to fill in before passing the account along. That adds work to the one
part of the process everyone's already trying to move past quickly, and it still
depends on someone remembering to fill it out completely. [The same pattern shows up
in ClickUp and Airtable falling out of sync](/blog/clickup-airtable-agency-stack-nobody-talks-about-replacing):
the information already exists somewhere, usually in a CRM or a signed proposal, and
the gap is that nothing connects that record to the tools the delivery team actually
works in.

## What a connected handoff looks like

When a deal closes, the scope, timeline, and stakeholder list should land directly
in the project record the delivery team opens on day one, not get retyped from
memory into a fresh ClickUp task. That's the same principle behind [onboarding new
clients without the spreadsheet chaos](/blog/onboarding-new-clients-without-the-spreadsheet-chaos):
day one goes faster when the record already has what the client told sales, and a
person still reviews it before kickoff instead of a form asking the client to repeat
themselves.

## Where this fits into the bigger picture

This is one more version of [automating the boring 80% of agency
ops](/blog/automating-the-boring-80-percent-agency-ops-framework): the handoff
itself, connecting one system's record to another's, is exactly the kind of
repeatable step that doesn't need a person doing it by hand every time. [See how
workflow design closes this gap](/services) before the first client question makes
it obvious it's still open.
