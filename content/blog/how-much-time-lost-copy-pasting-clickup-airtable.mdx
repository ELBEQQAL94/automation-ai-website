Nobody puts "update Airtable to match ClickUp" on their calendar. It's not a task, it's a
reflex - copy-pasting the same fields in the gaps between real work, five minutes here and ten
minutes there, for every active client. Because it never gets scheduled, it never gets
questioned. It just happens, quietly, all week long.

## The task that's everywhere and nowhere

Ask anyone on the team how much time they spend on manual sync and the honest answer is
usually "not much, a few minutes here and there." Ask them to actually total it across every
client, every status change, every new record, and the number is almost always bigger than
either of you expected. Small recurring tasks are the easiest kind to underestimate, because
no single instance of them feels worth timing.

## A rough estimate worth actually running

Pick one account and time it for a week: every time someone touches ClickUp and then has to
go touch Airtable to match, or the other way around. Multiply by the number of active
clients. Most agencies that do this exercise once never do it again, because the number is
uncomfortable enough to act on immediately.

## Why this task specifically resists prioritization

It never breaks anything dramatically - it just slowly drifts, a record here, a status
there, until someone notices the two tools disagree and has to reconcile them from memory.
That slow failure mode is exactly why it stays low priority. Nothing visibly breaks until the
data's already wrong.
