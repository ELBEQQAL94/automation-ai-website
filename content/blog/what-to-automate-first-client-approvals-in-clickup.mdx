If your agency already tracks client approvals as a stage in ClickUp, the instinct is to look
at that stage first when thinking about automation. It's usually the wrong place to start.
The approval decision needs a person. Everything touching that decision doesn't.

## Start with the notification, not the decision

The most common gap isn't "the client took too long to decide" - it's "nobody told the client
it was ready to review until someone remembered to." Automating the notification the moment a
task hits the approval stage removes a delay that has nothing to do with the client's actual
response time.

## Then automate the status update

Once approval happens, someone still has to move the task, update the record, and notify
whoever's next in line. None of that requires judgment - it's a status change that should
follow automatically from the approval itself, the way changing a light switch turns on a
light without a second step.

## Leave the approval exactly where it is

This is the core of [human-in-the-loop
automation](/blog/human-in-the-loop-automation-explained): automate the parts that don't need
a person, and leave the parts that do untouched. For a ClickUp-based approval flow, that means
the "approve" button stays exactly where it is. Everything before and after it is fair game.

## Where this shows up at scale
